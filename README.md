# Hybrid and Regenerative Retrofit on the World's Largest Machines

*A positioning review of known techniques, ordered by maturity.*

**Author:** Aaron Garcia · aaron@garcia.ltd
**Status:** Review / synthesis — **not** a research contribution. Public.

## What this is

A cross-machine review asking one practical question about the physically largest powered machines in industry: **where does hybrid or regenerative retrofit actually pay off today, and where is it still a research bet?**

Every technique discussed already exists in the literature. This paper claims **no novelty** — its only contribution is *breadth* (putting these machine classes on one page) and *candour* about the gap between what is deployable and what is not.

## What it concludes

- **The mature lever — regenerative energy recovery** on the swing/slew axis of cyclic, high-inertia machines (large hydraulic excavators) is deployable today (TRL 8–9) with off-the-shelf components, delivering double-digit efficiency gains. Extension to draglines and bucket-wheel excavators is a reasonable but **unquantified** extrapolation, flagged as such.
- **The heuristic** that started the work — a simple opportunity matrix ranking machines by retrofit payoff — is offered honestly as *intuition*, explicitly inferior to formal Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (AHP/TOPSIS), which already covers this ground.
- **The speculative frontier — ultra-scale magnetic gearing** (Atallah & Howe, 2001) is labelled TRL 2–3, with a large unproven scaling gap and a logical mismatch to a bearing-dominated failure mode. Included as an honest research bet, not a solution.
- **Reliability today** is bought with condition monitoring and redundancy, not with an air-gap coupling that does not yet exist at scale.

## Honesty notes
